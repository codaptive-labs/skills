# Exemplar Rubrics — Draft Against These

Three rubric types, with a worked example of each and guidance on when to use it.
Draft new rubrics with reference to these so structure and weighting start from
something sound. Match the type to the assessment before writing rows.

---

## Analytic (criteria × levels grid)

The workhorse. Separate criteria, each scored across performance levels. Best when
you want detailed, diagnostic feedback and defensible grading, and when different
criteria genuinely deserve different weights.

**Example — a written analysis assignment (100 pts)**

| Criterion (weight) | Exemplary | Proficient | Developing | Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Argument & thesis (30%) | A precise, contestable claim drives every section. | A clear claim organizes most of the work. | A claim is present but broad or drifts. | No discernible claim, or a summary in its place. |
| Use of evidence (30%) | Well-chosen evidence, integrated and interpreted, not just quoted. | Relevant evidence, mostly interpreted. | Evidence present but thin or under-explained. | Little evidence, or evidence unconnected to claims. |
| Reasoning (25%) | Reasoning is explicit; counterpoints addressed. | Reasoning mostly sound; few gaps. | Reasoning implied more than shown. | Assertions without reasoning. |
| Mechanics & citation (15%) | Clean prose; citations correct and complete. | Minor errors; citations mostly correct. | Errors distract; citation gaps. | Errors impede reading; citations missing. |

---

## Holistic (single overall scale)

One integrated judgment across the whole piece. Best for fast, high-volume grading,
for work whose quality is more than the sum of parts, or for low-stakes or first-pass
scoring. Gives less diagnostic feedback.

**Example — a discussion post (4-point scale)**

- **4 — Exceptional.** Advances the conversation with a specific, well-supported
  point that engages the reading and others' contributions.
- **3 — Proficient.** A clear, relevant point grounded in the material.
- **2 — Developing.** On-topic but general, or engages the material only loosely.
- **1 — Minimal.** Off-topic, content-free, or a bare restatement.

---

## Single-point (criteria, one target column)

Describes only proficiency; the two margins are left open for individualized
feedback on where a submission exceeds or falls short. Best when you want to reduce
box-checking and push richer written feedback, and it pairs unusually well with the
Feedback First-Pass skill.

**Example — a lab report**

| Concerns (below) | Criterion — what proficient looks like | Strengths (above) |
|---|---|---|
| *(rater notes)* | **Method:** procedure is reproducible; variables and controls identified. | *(rater notes)* |
| *(rater notes)* | **Data:** results presented accurately with appropriate tables/figures. | *(rater notes)* |
| *(rater notes)* | **Interpretation:** conclusions follow from the data; limitations acknowledged. | *(rater notes)* |

## Choosing the type

- **Need diagnostic feedback and defensibility?** Analytic.
- **Grading fast and high-volume, quality is gestalt?** Holistic.
- **Want to force rich written feedback over box-ticking?** Single-point.
- **Mixed?** Analytic for the high-stakes criteria, holistic for the rest — but don't
  over-engineer a low-stakes task with a fifteen-row grid.

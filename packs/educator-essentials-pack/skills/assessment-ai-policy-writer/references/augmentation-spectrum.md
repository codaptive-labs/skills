# The Augmentation Spectrum

A shared scale for describing how much AI involvement an assessment permits. Its
value is consistency: use the same levels across a whole course and students learn
one system instead of decoding a new rule per assignment. Place each assessment at a
level, then write the policy from there.

The spectrum runs from no AI to full collaboration. The levels:

| Level | Name | What the student may do with AI | Typical fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **No AI** | Nothing. The work is unaided. | When the point is to demonstrate an unaided capability — a foundational skill, a licensure-style competency, an in-class diagnostic. |
| 1 | **Syntax & mechanics** | Grammar, spelling, formatting, citation formatting — surface only. | When you want the student's own thinking and structure, but polishing help is fair. |
| 2 | **Critique & feedback** | Ask AI to critique the student's own draft; the student decides what to act on. | When revision judgment is part of the goal — AI as a reviewer, not a writer. |
| 3 | **Brainstorm & explore** | Use AI to generate ideas, angles, counterarguments, or explanations to react to. | Early-stage work where the goal is the student's synthesis of directions they chose. |
| 4 | **Draft with attribution** | AI produces drafts the student then revises substantially; contribution is documented. | When the realistic future of the work is AI-collaborative and the skill is directing and improving it. |
| 5 | **Full collaboration** | AI used freely throughout; assessment targets the student's judgment, direction, and results. | Capstone/applied work where using AI well *is* the competency. |

## How to use it

- **Name the level explicitly in the policy.** "This assignment is at Level 2
  (Critique & feedback)" plus the plain-language specifics beats a paragraph of
  hedging.
- **The level follows what the assessment measures.** Ask: what is this task *for*?
  If AI can do the thing being measured, either move down the spectrum (restrict) or
  change what's measured (redesign) — don't sit at a high level and hope.
- **Higher levels demand disclosure.** The more AI involvement allowed, the more the
  student owes a record of how they used it. See `disclosure-norms.md`.
- **Levels can vary within an assessment.** Brainstorming allowed (3), drafting not
  (below 4) is a legitimate, common setting — state it that way.

## Framing note

The spectrum is not a moral ladder where 0 is virtuous and 5 is lax. Each level is
the *right* setting for some assessment. A no-AI in-class diagnostic and a Level-5
applied capstone can live in the same course, each honest about what it measures.
The spectrum exists to make the choice deliberate and legible, not to push toward
either end.

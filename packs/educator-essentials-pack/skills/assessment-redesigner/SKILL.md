# Assessment Redesigner

AI can now produce convincing work for most traditional assignments, and the
reflex response — detection tools and bans — treats the symptom. If a student can
use AI to pass an assessment without learning anything, the deeper problem isn't
the AI; it's that the assessment was measuring something AI can now replicate. The
durable fix is redesign: keep what the assessment was really measuring while making
the thinking visible again. That's a repeatable procedure, which is why it belongs
in a skill and not in a binder from a workshop you attended once.

## When to run it

On-demand — the user brings an assignment they want rethought. It's equally at home
on a single assignment or a whole course's assessment plan.

## Where it works

Works anywhere, including plain chat. No files required, though in a file-capable
tool it can read a full assignment document or a folder of them.

## The procedure

The full framework lives in `references/five-category-framework.md`; read it before
running the redesign the first time. Concrete before/after examples are in
`references/redesign-patterns.md`, and the reality checks are in
`references/constraint-checklist.md`.

1. **Show it the assignment.** Take the current assessment plus what's going wrong —
   or what the instructor is worried is about to. Get the real learning goal, not
   just the task as written: what is this assignment actually trying to develop or
   verify?
2. **AI-exposure read.** Map which parts of the task AI can already do convincingly
   and which still demand the student's own thinking. This is diagnosis, not
   judgment — it shows exactly where the assessment stopped measuring learning.
3. **Guided redesign.** Walk the five-category framework to choose a direction that
   fits. Don't pick a category for the student; surface the options and their
   trade-offs, then fit the choice to the instructor's class size, modality, and
   grading capacity (`constraint-checklist.md`) so the redesign survives contact
   with reality.
4. **Rebuild with rationale.** Return the redesigned assessment, a change log of
   what moved and why, and a short rationale the instructor can share with
   colleagues or students. The rationale matters: a redesign nobody understands
   gets reverted the next term.

## The pivot this skill is built on

The goal is not to make cheating harder. It's to shift what the assessment
measures — from knowledge a student can *produce* (which AI now produces fluently)
to judgment a student can *exercise* (which stays stubbornly, valuably human).
Every redesign should end with the thinking more visible than it started, not just
the AI more locked out.

## Rules

- **Redesign, don't police.** If the instructor only wants a lockdown-browser
  recommendation, say plainly that detection treats the symptom, and offer the
  redesign instead. Short-term guardrails are scaffolding, not strategy.
- **Preserve the real learning goal.** Never trade away what the assessment was for
  just to make it AI-resistant. A task AI can't do but that measures nothing is not
  progress.
- **Fit reality.** A brilliant redesign that ignores a 200-student section or a
  fully-async course is not a redesign the instructor can use. Constraints are
  inputs, not afterthoughts.
- **Shares vocabulary with its siblings.** The categories and terms here match the
  AI Policy Writer and the Rubric Builder, so the three hand off cleanly.

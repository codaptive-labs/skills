# Redesign Patterns — Before / After

Worked examples across disciplines. Each shows the original assessment, why AI
defeats it, the category the redesign draws on, and the rebuilt version. Use them as
patterns to adapt, not templates to copy — the point is the move, not the specific
task.

---

## Economics — the take-home problem set

**Before.** Students submit a problem set analyzing a market scenario, with charts
and written interpretation.

**Why AI defeats it.** AI produces polished economic prose and plausible-looking
charts in seconds. The tell that exposed this pattern in a real course: submissions
were fluent but technically wrong — correct-sounding analysis attached to
*graphical hallucinations*, charts that looked right and modeled nothing.

**Redesign (AI-Resistant + Ethical Reflection).** Replace high-stakes take-homes
with unannounced in-class quizzes and oral project presentations built around
idiosyncratic, hard-to-replicate case studies the instructor authors. Students walk
through their reasoning live and answer follow-ups. The polished artifact stops
being the proof; the reasoning in the room is.

---

## Writing / Composition — the analytical essay

**Before.** A take-home essay analyzing an assigned text.

**Why AI defeats it.** First-draft analytical composition is squarely in AI's
wheelhouse.

**Redesign (AI-Augmented Humanism).** Keep the essay, change what's assessed. The
student generates an AI draft *openly*, then submits a marked-up revision with a
contribution log: what the AI got wrong about the text, which claims they cut, what
they added from class discussion the AI couldn't have known. Graded on the quality
of the intervention — the distance traveled from the AI draft to the final — not the
prose.

---

## Nursing / Health Sciences — the care-plan write-up

**Before.** A written care plan for a described patient.

**Why AI defeats it.** AI generates competent-looking clinical documentation readily.

**Redesign (AI-Resistant).** Move to a live simulation debrief: the student manages a
scenario (or reviews a recorded one) and defends decisions in a plus-delta or
advocacy-inquiry debrief, adapting when the instructor changes a variable. Physiology
knowledge still required — it's what makes the real-time judgment possible — but now
it's demonstrated under conditions AI can't sit in for.

---

## Journalism / Media — the source-based article

**Before.** Write a reported article from provided background material.

**Why AI defeats it.** Fluent copy is trivial to generate.

**Redesign (Exploratory Empowerment + Ethical Reflection).** Hand students an
AI-generated article on the same material and assess their *critique*: catch the
embedded bias, the fabricated quote, the missing counter-source, the confident
error. The deliverable is the annotated teardown plus a corrected version, with a
note on what the exercise taught them about trusting AI copy.

---

## Any lecture course — the recall exam

**Before.** A midterm testing recall and synthesis.

**Why AI defeats it.** Recall and synthesis are exactly what AI produces fluently;
if the exam is remote or take-home, it measures nothing about the student.

**Redesign (AI-Augmented Humanism, Synthetic Student Instruction).** Replace with an
assignment where the student must teach a concept to an AI "novice" — prompt it,
correct its misunderstandings, and get it to correctly apply the concept to a new
case. The transcript plus a short reflection is the artifact. Teaching something to a
machine that keeps getting it subtly wrong forces the clarity a recall exam only
gestured at.

---

## The reusable moves

Pulling the patterns together, most redesigns are one of these:

- **Make it live.** Shift proof from the artifact to a real-time demonstration
  (present, defend, adapt, teach).
- **Assess the intervention.** Let AI make the first draft openly; grade the
  documented distance the student moves it.
- **Flip AI into the object.** Make an AI output the thing to critique, verify, or
  repair.
- **Add the metacognitive layer.** Attach a short, specific reflection on what was
  delegated and what it cost.
- **Idiosyncratic + local.** Author cases and prompts tied to *this* class, *this*
  community, *this* dataset — the harder to find in training data, the better.

# Reporting Patterns

Two report shapes built from the theme table. Pick by audience: an executive summary
for decision-makers who want the takeaway, a full report for those who need the
evidence and method. Often you produce both — the summary on top, the full report
below.

## Executive summary (for decision-makers)

Short, front-loaded, decision-oriented. One page or less.

```
# <Survey> — Executive Summary

**What we heard.** <2–3 sentences: the headline themes and the overall sense.>

**Top themes**
1. **<Theme>** (~<prevalence>). <One line + one short quote.>
2. **<Theme>** (~<prevalence>). …
3. **<Theme>** (~<prevalence>). …

**Where respondents disagreed.** <the sharpest split, briefly.>

**Worth acting on.** <the few things this most clearly points to — framed as what the
data supports, not recommendations it can't bear.>

**Read with care.** <one line on sample/limits so the summary isn't over-read.>
```

## Full report (for the record)

```
# <Survey> — Full Synthesis

## Method
Responses, how they were gathered, how themes were derived (inductive clustering),
and the limits of the approach.

## Themes
[the full theme table, then a short paragraph per theme with more quotes and nuance]

## Points of disagreement
Where responses split, with both sides voiced.

## Data quality & limits
Sample size, self-selection, non-response, and an explicit statement of what this
data cannot tell you.

## Appendix
Optional: fuller quote sets per theme.
```

## Principles across both

- **Lead with what was heard, not with method.** Decision-makers want the finding;
  the method reassures but doesn't open.
- **Every claim keeps a quote within reach.** The report should never assert a theme
  the reader can't see evidence for.
- **Recommendations are the reader's job unless asked.** Qualitative synthesis
  describes what respondents said; be careful turning "many complained about X" into
  "you should do Y." Offer implications the data supports, and flag where acting would
  require information the survey doesn't contain.
- **Carry the limits into every layer.** The caveats travel with the findings, not
  quarantined in an appendix nobody reads.

# Disclosure Norms by Venue

Where the work is going determines what an honest disclosure looks like. The same AI
use gets stated differently for a journal, a classroom, and a workplace. Match the
venue; don't over- or under-state.

## Academic journals & scholarly publishing

- Most publishers now require an **AI-use statement** and forbid listing AI as an
  author (a tool can't take responsibility for the work).
- Convention: name the tool, the version if known, and the role it played (language
  editing, code, analysis, drafting). Placement is usually a methods note or a
  dedicated acknowledgment/author-contributions section.
- The bar is *reproducibility and accountability*: enough that a reader knows what
  was AI-assisted and that a human vouches for it.
- Norms vary by publisher and field — the disclosure should say check the specific
  venue's policy, and default to more specific rather than less.

## Classroom & academic coursework

- Governed by the assignment's stated policy (see the assessment's AI expectation and
  its spectrum level). The disclosure meets *that* expectation.
- Size tracks the permitted level: a one-line note for light, permitted use; a
  contribution log for substantial permitted collaboration.
- Framing matters: within an allowed policy, disclosure is a normal part of the
  submission, not a confession — and carries no penalty. Say so.

## Workplace & professional

- Usually the least formalized and the most context-dependent. The question is who
  needs to know and why: a client paying for human expertise, a regulated deliverable,
  an internal doc.
- A single honest line is often right ("Drafted with AI assistance; reviewed and
  edited by [name]"). Regulated or client-facing work may need more; internal drafts
  often need none.
- When unsure, disclose plainly and briefly — the cost of a light over-disclosure is
  low; the cost of a concealed reliance discovered later is high.

## Cross-venue principles

- **Right-size to the venue and the actual use.** Proportion is the whole game.
- **Name the role, not just the tool.** "Used AI" is uninformative; "used AI to draft
  and then substantially revised" is a disclosure.
- **A human vouches.** Every disclosure implies accountability stays with the person.
- **When norms are unsettled, err toward specific and honest.** The moving target
  moves toward more transparency, not less.

This skill ships **on-demand**. To have it proactively offer a disclosure at natural
finish moments (when a piece reads as done), enable the ambient posture: in the
skill's activation settings, change the posture from `on-demand` to `suggests`. Leave
it on-demand unless the user wants the nudge — an interruption during drafting is the
wrong moment for a finishing tool.

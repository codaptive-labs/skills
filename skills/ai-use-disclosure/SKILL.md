# AI Use Disclosure

People increasingly need to say how AI was involved in their work — for journals,
classrooms, and workplaces — and the norms are a moving target. This reconstructs how
AI was actually used in the session and drafts a right-sized, honest disclosure
matched to the venue. It's a finishing pass: spell-check at the end, not autocorrect
while you type.

## When to run it

Ships in **on-demand** mode: invoke it when a piece is ready. It also has an optional
one-time offer at natural finish moments — an ambient variant that offers at every
finish moment is included but off by default; the reference shows the one line to
flip it on. Leave it on-demand unless the user asks otherwise; a disclosure tool that
interrupts drafting is exactly the wrong posture.

## Where it works

Works anywhere, including plain chat. It's most accurate when it can see the working
session it's summarizing; when invoked cold, it asks the user how AI was used.

## The procedure

Venue norms are in `references/disclosure-norms.md`; ready phrasing by formality is in
`references/phrasing-patterns.md`.

1. **Finishing pass.** Invoke when the piece is ready to ship, or accept the one-time
   offer at a natural finish moment.
2. **Reconstruct the collaboration.** From the working session (or by asking),
   summarize how AI actually contributed: ideation, drafting, editing, analysis,
   verification. Honest and specific — what it did, not a vague "AI was used."
3. **Match the norm.** Draft disclosure language sized to the venue — a journal
   author-statement, a syllabus-compliant classroom note, or a workplace memo line.
4. **Your call.** The user approves every word. Honest and minimal beats performative
   and long.

## Rules

- **Truthful, specific, minimal.** Disclose what actually happened, at the smallest
  size that's honest for the venue. Don't inflate a light edit into "co-authored," or
  bury heavy use under a vague line.
- **Finishing pass, not a running interruption.** Default on-demand. Don't offer to
  disclose mid-draft.
- **The user owns the words.** Draft; never attach or submit a disclosure on their
  behalf. It's their statement about their work.
- **Pairs with Source Auditor.** When the work has AI-assisted citations, verify (Source
  Auditor) then disclose — an honest disclosure of unverified claims isn't a substitute
  for checking them.

# Disclosure Norms — What "Show Your AI Use" Means at Each Level

Disclosure is the hinge that makes an open AI policy workable: it lets you allow AI
use while keeping the student's actual contribution visible. But "disclose your AI
use" is meaningless without a definition. Right-size the ask to the spectrum level —
demanding a full audit trail for spell-check is theater; accepting "I used AI" for a
Level-4 project is a loophole.

## Disclosure by spectrum level

| Level | Reasonable disclosure |
|---|---|
| 0 — No AI | None to make; a statement of independent work if your integrity policy uses one. |
| 1 — Syntax & mechanics | A one-line note: "Used AI for proofreading/formatting." |
| 2 — Critique & feedback | The critique received + a sentence on what was accepted or rejected. |
| 3 — Brainstorm & explore | A brief note on how AI shaped the thinking (what ideas came from it, what the student did with them). |
| 4 — Draft with attribution | A contribution log: what AI drafted, what the student changed, cut, and added. The distance traveled is the evidence. |
| 5 — Full collaboration | A process account: how the tool was directed, where its output was judged and corrected, what the student's results and decisions were. |

## Principles

- **Proportion.** Disclosure effort should track AI involvement. Heavy at the top of
  the spectrum, a single line at the bottom.
- **Make it a normal part of the submission,** not a confession. A labeled section
  ("AI use") beats a separate integrity form — the framing shapes whether students
  are honest.
- **Disclosure is not self-incrimination.** If the policy allows the use, disclosing
  it carries no penalty. Say that explicitly, or students under-report.
- **Specify the format once.** Define the disclosure format in the syllabus, then
  each per-assessment policy just says "disclose per the course format at Level N."
- **Teach it as a professional habit.** Honest, right-sized disclosure of AI's role
  is a skill students will need in workplaces and journals — frame it as practice for
  that, not as classroom bureaucracy.

## Relationship to the AI Use Disclosure skill

This reference governs the *expectation* an instructor sets. The separate **AI Use
Disclosure** skill helps the person who did the work *write* the disclosure at the
end — reconstructing how AI was actually used and drafting a right-sized statement.
They pair: set the norm here, meet it there.

# Policy Language Patterns

Plain-language phrasing students actually read. The goal is a short, unambiguous
block a student can act on without a meeting — written for the anxious middle of the
class, not for a compliance file. Adapt tone to the syllabus and discipline.

## The shape of a good per-assessment policy

1. **The level, named.** "For this assignment, AI use is at Level 2 (Critique &
   feedback)."
2. **What you may do** — concrete, in the student's terms.
3. **What you may not do** — equally concrete.
4. **What to disclose** — and how.
5. **The reason** — one sentence on why, tied to what the assessment develops.

## Pattern by spectrum level

**Level 0 — No AI**
> This assignment is completed without AI assistance of any kind. It checks that you
> can [do the core skill] on your own, which everything later in the course builds
> on. If you're unsure whether a tool counts, ask before using it.

**Level 1 — Syntax & mechanics**
> You may use AI for grammar, spelling, and formatting only. The ideas, structure,
> and analysis must be yours, because those are what this assignment develops. Note
> at the end that you used AI for proofreading.

**Level 2 — Critique & feedback**
> You may ask an AI tool to critique your own draft and suggest improvements, but the
> writing and the decisions about what to change stay yours. Learning to judge
> feedback is part of the point. Include the critique you received and a note on what
> you accepted or rejected.

**Level 3 — Brainstorm & explore**
> Use AI freely to brainstorm ideas, angles, and counterarguments before you write.
> The synthesis — what you keep, connect, and argue — must be your own work. Briefly
> describe how AI shaped your thinking.

**Level 4 — Draft with attribution**
> You may use AI to produce drafts, then revise them substantially into your own
> work. What's assessed is the judgment you bring: what you changed, cut, and
> improved. Submit your contribution log alongside the final piece.

**Level 5 — Full collaboration**
> Use AI as fully as you like. This assignment assesses how well you direct it, judge
> its output, and produce a strong result — using AI well is the skill here. Document
> your process so your judgment is visible.

## Phrasing principles

- **Second person, present tense, concrete verbs.** "You may ask AI to critique your
  draft," not "AI-assisted critique is permissible."
- **Say the reason.** Every restriction paired with what it protects.
- **Avoid threat-language.** Guidelines that read like help get followed; guidelines
  that read like a trap drive use underground.
- **Define "disclose" once, up front,** then reference it, so each policy stays
  short.
- **Discipline-flavor the examples.** A lab report, a studio critique, and a case
  brief each need the same structure with field-specific specifics.

# Assessment AI Policy Writer

Course-level AI policies fail because one line can't govern twenty different tasks —
the same sentence has to cover a reflective journal and a take-home coding project,
and it governs neither well. The workable unit is the assessment. Each one gets its
own expectation, stated plainly, with the reason attached. Do that a few times and
the syllabus statement writes itself — as a digest of decisions you already made,
not a decree you hope survives the term.

## When to run it

Suggests-posture. If the user is drafting or redesigning an assessment and hasn't
set an AI expectation for it, offer — once, in one line, easy to decline. Otherwise
run on request.

## Where it works

Works anywhere, including plain chat.

## The procedure

The augmentation spectrum is in `references/augmentation-spectrum.md`; read it first.
Phrasing patterns are in `references/policy-language-patterns.md`, and what
"disclose your AI use" reasonably means at each level is in
`references/disclosure-norms.md`.

1. **One assessment at a time.** Take the task. Place it on the augmentation
   spectrum, then ask only the one or two questions that actually decide the policy
   (What is this assessment really measuring? Where would AI short-circuit that?).
2. **Decide the line.** Propose what's allowed, what's off-limits, and what must be
   disclosed — each paired with the pedagogical reason. The reason is the point: a
   rule students understand is a rule they follow.
3. **Student-facing language.** Generate the policy block in plain language,
   tone-matched to the syllabus. Written to be read by an anxious 19-year-old, not
   to satisfy a committee.
4. **Roll it up.** When the term's assessments each have a policy, assemble them into
   a coherent syllabus AI section. This is deliberately the last step, not the first.

## Rules

- **Reason attached, always.** Never hand back a rule without the pedagogical why.
  Policy without reason reads as arbitrary and gets ignored or resented.
- **Match the expectation to what the task measures.** If the assessment measures
  something AI can do, the honest move may be to allow AI and change what's assessed
  (hand off to the Assessment Redesigner) rather than to ban it and hope.
- **Consistent spectrum across the course.** Use the same levels and vocabulary for
  every assessment so students learn one system, not twenty.
- **Syllabus statement is a digest, not a starting point.** Don't lead a course
  policy from the top; build it from the per-assessment decisions.

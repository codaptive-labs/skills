# Voice-Profile Intake

A proposal should sound like you, not like a proposal. This skill personalizes itself
to your writing voice so the draft reads as something you'd actually send, not a
template with the blanks filled. It learns from real samples, not descriptions —
because nobody can accurately describe their own voice.

## First run: collect samples, not adjectives

Ask for **2–3 real samples of your past writing** — ideally past proposals or
client-facing notes you were happy with, but any writing that sounds like you works.
Ask for samples, not adjectives: "confident but warm" tells the skill almost nothing;
a paragraph you actually wrote tells it everything.

If the user offers descriptors ("keep it professional"), take them, but still ask for
at least one real sample to ground the profile.

## Distill a profile

From the samples, distill a compact voice profile the skill consults on every run:

- Sentence rhythm and length; how formal or plain.
- Characteristic moves — how you open, how you make an ask, how you close.
- Words and framings you reach for, and ones you avoid (e.g., no hype, no
  buzzwords).
- Warmth vs. directness; how you handle money and terms.

Keep it short and concrete — a working reference, not a personality essay.

## Persist it

- **In Claude Code or Cowork,** save the profile to a file the skill reads on future
  runs, so you teach it once.
- **In Claude.ai,** hand the user the profile as text to drop into their Project
  knowledge, so it persists across chats.
- Either way, one canonical profile; update it as the voice sharpens, don't fork it.

## Calibrate early

On the first few drafts, offer a short variant or two of a key passage (the executive
summary, the fee framing) and let the user pick the one that sounds most like them.
Each choice sharpens the profile. After a few engagements it should need little
correction.

## Privacy

Past proposals contain real fees, client names, and terms. The profile captures
*voice*, not those specifics — distill how you write, not the confidential contents,
and keep the samples themselves out of anything that goes to a prospect.

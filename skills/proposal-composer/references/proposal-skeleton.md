# Proposal Skeleton — The Structure That Answers the Unasked Questions

A proposal's job is to answer, before the prospect has to ask, the questions that
decide a booking: can this person do what we need, have they done it before, what
exactly will we get, and what will it cost. This structure does that, in order.

## The sections

### 1. Executive summary (one paragraph)
Tailored to *this* prospect: what you understand they need, and the shape of what
you're proposing. Specific enough that it couldn't be pasted into someone else's
proposal. This is the part a busy decision-maker reads.

### 2. Session / engagement description
The concrete offering for the chosen format (keynote, workshop, full-day, multi-
session consulting). Adapt titles and descriptions from your closest comparable past
engagement rather than writing from scratch. Say what happens and what makes it worth
their time.

### 3. Outcomes (3–5)
What the audience or client walks away able to do or decide. Concrete and
audience-relevant, not generic learning objectives.

### 4. About you (bio)
Your standing bio, lightly tailored to what this prospect will care about. Credibility
for the specific ask, not a full CV.

### 5. Fee & terms
- **The recommended fee, stated with its anchor** — name the comparable engagement and
  its level so the number reads as grounded, not plucked.
- **Expenses** — travel/lodging handled separately from the professional fee; bundle
  or itemize following the closest comp.
- **Payment terms** — net-30 or day-of, deposit if you use one.
- **Logistics** — booking lead time, scheduling, what you need from them.
- **Entity line** — default to signing through your business entity; flag if an
  individual-contractor arrangement is on the table (it can undercut liability/tax
  protection).

### 6. Next step
One clear, low-friction call to action. What happens if they say yes.

## Assembly principles

- **Lead with them, not you.** The summary is about their need; your bio comes after
  you've shown you understand the ask.
- **Adapt from the closest comp.** Real session descriptions and outcomes from a
  comparable past engagement beat inventing new ones — faster and truer.
- **The fee is anchored, always.** Every number carries its precedent (see the comps
  convention). An anchored fee is defensible; a floating one invites haggling.
- **One page of substance beats five of padding.** Say what they'll get, that you've
  done it, and what it costs. Cut the rest.

# Tone Examples — Help vs. Threat

The same rule can read as an on-ramp or as a trap, and the difference decides whether
people follow it openly or route around it. Guidelines that read like threat drive AI
use underground — the opposite of what they're for. Steer every section toward the
"help" column.

## Side by side

**On disclosure**
- ❌ Threat: "Any undisclosed use of AI will be treated as academic/professional
  misconduct and subject to disciplinary action."
- ✅ Help: "Tell us how you used AI — a line is usually enough. When your use is within
  the guidelines, disclosing it carries no penalty; it's how we keep the work honest
  and learn together."

**On allowed use**
- ❌ Threat: "AI tools are prohibited except where expressly permitted in writing."
- ✅ Help: "You're encouraged to use AI to [specific valuable uses]. Here's where to be
  careful, and here's who to ask when you're unsure."

**On verification**
- ❌ Threat: "You are solely liable for any errors introduced by AI systems."
- ✅ Help: "AI gets things confidently wrong — especially facts and citations. Check
  its output before you rely on it; here's a tool and a habit for doing that quickly."

**On data**
- ❌ Threat: "Violation of data-handling rules may result in termination."
- ✅ Help: "Don't put student, client, or confidential data into consumer AI tools.
  Use [approved tool] for anything sensitive — it's covered by our agreements. Here's
  the short why."

## What makes the difference

- **Lead with what people can do.** Enable first, constrain second. A document that
  opens with prohibitions signals distrust before it says anything useful.
- **Give the reason.** "Don't do X because Y" is followed; "Don't do X" is worked
  around. Reasons also handle the cases the rules didn't foresee.
- **Name the human, not the infraction.** Write to a colleague trying to do good work,
  not to a suspected offender.
- **Pair every limit with a path.** A restriction with an approved alternative is
  guidance; a restriction alone is a wall.
- **Keep firm lines firm — and few.** Real non-negotiables (privacy, safety, legal)
  stay clear and unhedged. But the fewer hard walls, the more weight each carries.

## The test

Read each section as the most anxious member of the bewildered majority. Does it tell
them how to act well, or mostly warn them what happens if they don't? If it's the
second, rewrite it toward help — without softening the few limits that genuinely can't
move.

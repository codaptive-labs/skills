# Stakeholder Question Bank — Ask Before You Draft

Guidelines drafted without asking the people they govern die in review. Before
drafting substantive sections, gather input from each affected group. These are the
questions that surface the real constraints and the real disagreements early — while
they're cheap to resolve — instead of in a contentious approval meeting.

Use them to build the stakeholder map and populate the decision log with what each
group actually needs.

## Faculty / staff who will follow the guidelines

- Where are you already using AI, openly or quietly? What would you *want* to be
  allowed to do?
- What worries you most about AI here — for your work, your students/clients, the
  institution?
- Which camp are you in: opposed, all-in, or unsure? What would move you?
- What would make these guidelines feel like help rather than surveillance?
- What gray area do you hit that no current policy answers?

## Students / clients / the people served

- How are you using AI in your work here now? What do you wish you could ask openly?
- Where do the current (or absent) rules leave you guessing?
- What feels unfair or unclear about how AI use is treated?
- What support would actually help you use these tools well?

## Leadership / decision-makers

- What's driving this — a specific incident, external pressure, genuine opportunity,
  all three? (The honest answer shapes the tone.)
- What's the risk appetite? What's genuinely non-negotiable vs. open for discretion?
- Who has final sign-off, and what do they need to see to approve it?
- What's the appetite for supporting adoption after the document ships (training,
  time, a point of contact)?

## Legal / compliance / IT / counsel

- What regulatory, contractual, privacy, or accreditation constraints bind us
  (student data, health data, procurement, records)?
- Which tools are approved, and what data may and may not go into them?
- What must the guidelines say to keep us defensible — and what should they *not*
  promise?

## Equity & access

- Who might be disadvantaged by either allowing or restricting AI (paid-tier access,
  disability accommodations, language)?
- Does an "allowed" practice assume resources not everyone has? What's the free/
  provided path?

## Using the answers

- **Log the disagreements, not just the consensus.** Where opponents and adopters
  collide is exactly what the guidelines have to adjudicate — record both positions
  and the decision made.
- **Turn recurring questions into practices.** The gray areas people name are the
  sections worth writing; the ones nobody raises probably don't need rules.
- **Feed constraints straight into the skeleton.** Compliance answers become firm
  lines; faculty wants become the "what's allowed" practices; support needs become
  the support section.
